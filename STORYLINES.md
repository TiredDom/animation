# Kip, episode 1: three storylines

Pitched 2026-10-30. **Picked: D, Kip vs. Spectator Mode** (the user's own idea, at the end of this file);
A–C stay here for later episodes. The picked one becomes SPEC.md once the user locks the story.

## The format all three share

Kip's catchphrase is **"Easy."** He says it with total confidence about an everyday mission, and the
episode shows why it wasn't. Titles run "Kip vs. ___". Every episode ends on a gag that points to the next one.

Assumptions for all three: about 4–6 minutes, set by what the story needs to stay clear (no fixed length);
16:9 at 1920×1080, 30 fps; Kip talks with an ElevenLabs voice on the Creator plan (no extra cost; voice
options come once a story is picked); score from our orchestra engine and real SFX from the bank (free,
Content-ID safe). Before locking the pick, check its angle against what is already out there.

## A. Kip vs. the 3 AM Snack (recommended: one dark house, one goal, a gag every few seconds, and the simplest to make look great)

**Device and form:** a midnight mission staged like a stealth game. Top-down "floor plan" shots of the dark
house, where the spots that would make a noise glow, cut against tight close-ups: bare feet on a stair,
the cat's eyes, his hand on the fridge handle.

**What you see and feel:** At 3:07 AM Kip wakes up starving and whispers us his foolproof route to the last
pudding cup. Every step meets a new enemy (the creaky stair, the cat's bell, Dad's snore stopping, a
motion-sensor light), and in the kitchen a second shadow is already at the fridge: Mom, on the same mission.

**First 2 seconds:** his face lit blue by his phone reading 3:07. His stomach growls so loud the frame
shakes. He whispers: "Fridge. Thirty-seven steps. Easy."

**Beats:** hook → the route on the floor plan, planting three things (the creaky fourth stair, a cat treat
in his pocket, his rule "if Dad sees you, you're sleepwalking") → the stairs: he skips the creak, but the
cat blocks the hall → the treat gets him past → Dad's snore stops; he freezes on one foot until it starts
again → the kitchen: the fridge light floods the room and Mom is already there, holding the pudding
(midpoint: now it's a two-person heist with one pudding) → whispered negotiations, then the chip bag
crinkles → low point: the motion light snaps on, the cat knocks a pan, Dad's door opens → payoff: Kip and
Mom "sleepwalk" past Dad, arms out, snacks in hand (his planted rule) → back in bed he opens the chips;
from the dark: "...Kip." → end-screen gag: the next night, a padlock on the fridge.

**Look:** props only on a deep-blue night floor (stairs, rug, fridge, a hallway door), moonlight from one
side, the fridge as the one warm light; Kip's yellow hoodie is the brightest thing in every frame.

**Sound:** sneaky pizzicato strings that cut dead at every noise; foley up front (creaks, the cat's bell,
the crinkly bag as the running gag); Kip whispers throughout.

**New cast:** Mom, Dad and a cat in Kip's style.

## B. Kip vs. Grandma's New Phone (the most heart)

**Device and form:** Kip does the work, and the midpoint flips it. An object-to-object chain: every scene
ends on the phone's screen, which becomes the next scene (her blurry selfie turns into the photo on the
fridge; a voice message turns into the next morning).

**What you see and feel:** Mom sends Kip to teach Grandma her first smartphone in one afternoon, and every
lesson goes sideways: all-caps texts, forty photos of her thumb, an accidental video call to the whole
family. Halfway through, Kip catches her editing a video like a pro: she has known how all along and played
dumb so he would stay longer.

**First 2 seconds:** a video call fills the screen, showing only an extreme close-up of Grandma's ear:
"KIP? IS THIS THING ON?" Cut to Kip: "...Easy."

**Beats:** hook → the mission and "Easy." → texting lesson (ALL CAPS, emoji panic) → photo lesson (her
thumb), while Kip keeps checking the time because his friends' game starts at six (plant) → the family
video-call disaster → midpoint: from the hallway, Kip sees her typing at lightning speed → he confronts her;
she admits "You only come over when something needs fixing" → low point: it lands on him → payoff: he
drops his phone in the cookie tin she keeps offering (plant) and asks her to teach him something (her
pancake flip); they film it → it blows up → end-screen gag: her video beats all of his, and she says
"Easy."

**Look:** Grandma's warm daytime living room (armchair, doily, a plant, the cookie tin), soft window light;
the phone shown as clean screen inserts.

**Sound:** playful clarinet and bassoon; message pings and the camera shutter as punctuation; Grandma gets
her own ElevenLabs voice.

**New cast:** Grandma (Mom only on the phone).

## C. Kip vs. the Road Trip (the bold form)

**Device and form:** a broken rule, told in one frame that rebuilds. A fixed wide shot through the
windshield of the family car: inside, almost nothing moves, while outside the windows the world keeps
changing (city, fields, desert, night), cut with close inserts of the GPS screen.

**What you see and feel:** Dad sets one rule for the six-hour drive to the lake: nobody asks "are we there
yet". Kip invents sillier and sillier ways not to ask, while we notice the same cow pass the window again
and again: Dad has been lost for hours.

**First 2 seconds:** Dad's finger in Kip's face: "One rule. Nobody asks 'are we there yet.'" Kip: "Easy."
Smash to a card, "4 HOURS LATER": Kip upside down in the back seat, staring at the roof.

**Beats:** hook → games to kill time; counting cows, one with a heart-shaped spot (plant), and Mom asleep
in the passenger seat (plant) → boredom wins; Kip's workarounds ("are we... nearer than before?") → the
heart cow passes again (we see it, Kip doesn't) → midpoint: Kip spots the cow a third time, and Dad's face
says it all: they're lost, and now Dad can't admit it, so the bet flips → low point: fuel light, night,
everyone silent → payoff: Kip finds the paper map in the glovebox and follows the heart cow's farm sign →
sunrise over the lake → end-screen gag: Mom wakes up as they park: "Are we there yet?"

**Look:** the family car interior (seats, dashboard, windows), with the outside built as simple layered
landscapes sliding past, recoloured by the time of day; Kip's yellow hoodie against the grey car.

**Sound:** an easygoing acoustic road-trip theme, the turn signal's tick as the comedic metronome, the
radio cutting in and out.

**New cast:** Mom and Dad.

## D. Kip vs. Spectator Mode (PICKED: the user's idea, 2026-10-30)

The user: "What if Kip can do spectator mode", then "this will be a rebrand… lets revolve on kip". The premise
was the What If Grindset plan's opener; with the channel rebranded around Kip, it is Kip's.

**Device and form:** Kip does the work, and a midpoint reveal flips it. A flight through one world: the house is
one connected set. Spectator shots glide through walls, floors and ceilings like a game's spectator camera, while
the normal world is shot still and grounded. Two camera languages, one rule.

**What you see and feel:** On the night before his birthday, Kip dies in his video game, presses "Spectate" and
floats out of his own body, invisible and able to fly through walls, so finding where Mom hid his present is
"Easy." While he searches, we see what he doesn't: Mom finds his frozen body, smiles a little too knowingly,
draws a mustache on it and carries it upstairs. Halfway through, a see-through Mom floats out of a wall beside
him: she has had spectator mode for years, which is how moms always know everything, and with his respawn timer
running out, Kip has to find his own body before he is stuck as a ghost forever.

**First 2 seconds:** the TV reads "YOU DIED · SPECTATE? Y / N". Kip mashes Y, and a see-through Kip pops up out of
his real body, which stays frozen on the couch with the controller in its hands. (The thumbnail's moment.)

**Plants → payoffs:**
1. The TV: "SPECTATING · RESPAWN IN 5:00" → the second half's ticking clock.
2. The cat stares straight at ghost Kip and hisses (only the cat can see him) → the cat leads him to his body.
3. Mom on the phone: "I hid his present where he'll never look" → it was under his own bed.
4. Mom, also on the phone: "and he thinks I don't know about the chips under the couch" → she has spectator mode.
5. Mom moves his body and draws the mustache (the viewer sees, Kip doesn't) → his body isn't where he left it,
   and he wakes up on his birthday with a mustache.
6. Dad's disco photo in the closet, curly mustache and all (added with the spec) → in the morning mirror Kip
   sees his drawn-on mustache: "...I look like Dad."

**Beats:**
1. Hook: "SPECTATE?", Y, he pops out of his body; the respawn timer starts at 5:00.
2. He tests it: his hand through the lamp, his head through the wall; the cat hisses right at him.
3. The kitchen: Mom on the phone (plants 3 and 4). Kip, to us: "Find the present? Easy."
4. Search 1, Mom's closet: Dad's old disco photos, no present. Cut away: Mom finds the frozen body, smiles,
   draws a mustache, carries him upstairs.
5. Search 2, the garage: a wrapped box. He can't open it, so he sticks his head inside: socks, Grandma's present
   for Dad (a decoy).
6. Search 3, the attic: his head inside a trunk, someone taps his shoulder. Impossible.
7. Midpoint: a see-through Mom: "Looking for something?" She has had it for years ("How do you think I know about
   the chips?"); spectators can see each other; "Miss your respawn and you stay like this." Timer: 1:30.
8. He races back to the couch: his body is gone. The search flips to finding himself, past Mom's own body frozen
   mid-sip of tea, while ghost Mom floats along: "Warmer… colder…"
9. Low point, 0:15 left: he slumps on the stairs. "I just wanted to see my present."
10. Payoff: the cat meows and leads him to his room; his body is in his bed. He dives in at 0:01 and gasps
    awake… with a mustache.
11. Birthday morning: "It was under your bed the whole time." He slept on top of it all night.
12. End-screen gag: Kip and the cat stare at each other; a see-through cat floats out of the cat and glides
    through the wall. Next episode.

**Look:** Kip's house at night as one connected set (living room, kitchen, closet, garage, attic, his room), warm
lamp light. Ghost Kip is a translucent, softly glowing blue copy of himself, with no outlines. Spectator shots
carry a small game-style readout: "SPECTATING · RESPAWN 4:59".

**Sound:** a playful pizzicato-and-clarinet score; in spectator mode the room goes muffled, as if underwater, with
a soft whoosh on every wall pass; the respawn timer ticks louder as it runs out. Kip and Mom are voiced with
ElevenLabs.

**New cast:** Mom and the cat (Dad only in the closet photos).
